## 1. GDP Impact Projections

### 1.1 NFAP October 2025 Report -- Restriction Scenario

**Source:** National Foundation for American Policy, "Economic Impact of the Trump Administration Immigration Policies," October 2025.
**Methodology:** NFAP used Congressional Budget Office (CBO) baseline projections for GDP growth, then modeled the impact of specific immigration policy changes (deportations, visa restrictions, TPS terminations, refugee suspension) on labor force participation and GDP.

**(CITED PROJECTION)** Key GDP projections from NFAP:
- **Short-term (2025-2028):** Cumulative GDP reduction of $1.9 trillion, or $5,612 per person
- **Long-term (2025-2035):** Cumulative GDP reduction of $12.1 trillion, or $34,369 per person
- **Annual growth rate:** Drops from projected 1.8% average to 1.3% average (a one-third reduction)
- **2025 alone:** GDP growth projected to drop from 2.1% to 1.5%

**Important caveat from NFAP:** These estimates do not account for the likely additional economic drag from restricting high-skilled foreign workers, which economists say has historically driven up to half of U.S. productivity growth. The $12.1T figure may therefore be conservative.

Sources:
- [NFAP: Economic Impact of Trump Administration Immigration Policies (PDF)](https://nfap.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Economic-Impact-of-The-Trump-Administration-Immigration-Policies.NFAP-Policy-Brief.2025-1.pdf)
- [Fortune: Trump immigration policies would slash workforce](https://fortune.com/2025/10/21/trump-immigration-policy-jobs-workforce-american-workers-gdp-growth/)
- [The Hill: Trump immigration crackdown may eliminate 15M workers by 2035](https://thehill.com/homenews/administration/5561944-trump-immigration-impact-us-economy/)

### 1.2 Hoover Institution -- Reform/Expansion Scenario

**Source:** Hoover Institution, "Additional H-1B Workers Would Add Billions to GDP and Federal Tax Revenue."
**Methodology:** Modeled the economic impact of the Senate Gang of Eight immigration plan (S. 744), which proposed raising the H-1B general cap to 110,000-180,000 (from 65,000) and the master's degree cap from 20,000 to 25,000.

**(CITED PROJECTION)** Key findings:
- **GDP gain:** $456 billion over ten years from new H-1B workers
- **Accruing to current U.S. citizens/residents:** $244 billion of that total
- **Federal tax revenue:** $113 billion over ten years
- **Net cost of the reform plan:** ~$17 billion in appropriations over ten years

**Note:** The $456B figure reflects a specific policy proposal (Gang of Eight bill) and should not be directly compared to NFAP's $12.1T restriction estimate, which covers all immigration categories, not just H-1B.

Source: [Hoover Institution: Additional H-1B Workers Would Add Billions to GDP](https://www.hoover.org/research/additional-h-1b-workers-would-add-billions-gdp-and-federal-tax-revenue)

### 1.3 Brookings Institution -- Near-Term GDP and Consumer Spending

**Source:** Brookings Institution, "Macroeconomic Implications of Immigration Flows in 2025 and 2026," January 2026 Update.
**Methodology:** Scenario analysis based on immigration flow estimates and their impact on labor supply, GDP growth, and consumer spending.

**(CITED PROJECTION)** Key findings:
- **GDP growth reduction in 2025:** Between 0.2 and 0.3 percentage points
- **GDP growth reduction in 2026:** Between 0.1 and 0.3 percentage points
- **Consumer spending reduction in 2025:** $40-$60 billion less than 2024
- **Consumer spending reduction in 2026:** Additional $10-$40 billion
- **Cumulative two-year consumer spending impact:** $60-$110 billion

**(SOURCED)** Net migration turned negative in 2025 for the first time in at least 50 years -- estimated at between -295,000 and -10,000 for the year.

Sources:

## 3. Historical Precedent: The 1920s Immigration Restriction

### 3.1 The Immigration Act of 1924 and Innovation

**Source:** Petra Moser and Shmuel San, "Immigration, Science, and Invention: Lessons from the Quota Acts," working paper presented at AEA 2020; published research from NYU.
**Methodology:** Analyzed over 80,000 biographical records of American scientists and patent data in Eastern and Southern European (ESE) scientific fields before and after the 1924 quota enactment.

**(SOURCED)** Key findings from Moser and San:
- **Immigration volume decline:** Immigration from Eastern and Southern Europe plunged dramatically after 1924 quotas
- **Decline in U.S. invention in ESE fields:** 68% reduction
- **Decline in native-born U.S. scientist patents in ESE fields:** 45% fewer patents produced by American-born scientists
- **Fields with zero patents:** 16% additional ESE fields had no patents at all after quotas
- **Missing scientists:** An estimated 1,170 ESE-born scientists were "missing" from U.S. science by the 1950s
- **Duration of effect:** Damaging effects on U.S. invention persisted into the 1960s -- approximately 40 years

**Critical insight:** The decline was not limited to immigrant scientists themselves. Native-born American scientists working in the same fields also became dramatically less productive, demonstrating that immigrant scientists generate positive spillover effects for their domestic colleagues.

Sources:
- [Moser & San: Immigration, Science and Invention (AEA)](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2020/preliminary/paper/NydKa4TK)
- [Moser & San: SSRN working paper](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3558718)
- [Stanford GSB: The Brain Gain](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/brain-gain-impact-immigration-american-innovation)

### 3.2 Complementary Evidence from Related Research

**Source:** Davison, Doran, and Yoon, "Labor and Invention as Complements: Evidence from 1920s Immigration Quotas," January 2025 working paper, University of Notre Dame.

This paper provides complementary evidence that immigration and domestic invention function as complements, not substitutes -- restricting one diminishes the other.

Source: [Davison, Doran & Yoon (Notre Dame, Jan 2025)](https://sites.nd.edu/kirk-doran/files/2025/01/DavisonDoranYoon_Quota_Jan2025.pdf)

### 3.3 Broader Context of the 1920s Precedent

**(SOURCED)** Immigration volume data:
- Pre-1921: approximately 360,000 immigrants per year
- Post-1924 quotas: approximately 165,000 immigrants per year
- The effects of the restriction on invention persisted until the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965 liberalized the system

**Parallel to today:** Moser and San's findings suggest that current proposals to restrict immigration could trigger a similar multi-decade decline in American innovation capacity.

## 5. Counter-Arguments with Evidence Ratings

### METHODOLOGY FOR EVIDENCE RATINGS

Each claim is evaluated against peer-reviewed research and data from nonpartisan institutions. Ratings use a three-tier system:
- **LARGELY FALSE** (Green): The weight of peer-reviewed evidence contradicts the claim
- **MIXED** (Yellow): Some supporting evidence exists, but the claim oversimplifies or is true only in narrow circumstances
- **LARGELY TRUE** (Red): The weight of peer-reviewed evidence supports the claim

### 5.1 Claim: "H-1B Workers Displace American Workers"

**Rating: LARGELY FALSE**

**(SOURCED)** Richmond Federal Reserve Bank (2025, Economic Brief EB 25-39):
- "No evidence that hiring H-1B workers displaces native college-educated workers"
- Small, high-productivity firms that hire H-1B workers "hire more native graduates once they bring in immigrant talent"
- H-1B workers perform complementary tasks that "raise demand for natives overall"
- Native college graduates may face "some wage pressure" in heavily H-1B-dependent occupations, but these costs are "outweighed by the broader benefits immigration creates"

**(SOURCED)** National Academies of Sciences (2017): Long-term impact of immigration on native-born wages is "very small" over 10+ year periods.

**(SOURCED)** Kerr and Lincoln (NBER): Higher H-1B admissions did not displace American workers and increased U.S. patenting.

**Nuance:** While aggregate displacement is not supported by evidence, specific firm-level abuses exist (e.g., outsourcing companies using H-1B workers at below-market wages). The solution indicated by the evidence is enforcement reform, not program elimination.

Sources:
- [Richmond Fed: Understanding the Potential Impact of H-1B Visa Program Changes](https://www.richmondfed.org/publications/research/economic_brief/2025/eb_25-39)
- [Northwestern Kellogg: Does the H-1B Visa Program Hurt American Workers?](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/does-the-h1-b-visa-program-hurt-american-workers)

### 5.2 Claim: "Immigrants Are a Fiscal Burden"

**Rating: LARGELY FALSE**

**(CITED PROJECTION)** Cato Institute (2026, White Paper by Alex Nowrasteh):
- Immigrants generated a cumulative fiscal surplus of **$14.5 trillion** from 1994 to 2023 (in real 2024 dollars)
- Includes **$3.9 trillion** in savings on interest on the debt
- Without immigrants, U.S. government public debt at all levels would be at least **205% of GDP** -- nearly twice its 2023 level
- Immigrants accounted for **14% of tax revenue** but only **7% of government spending** (1994-2023)
- Per capita, immigrants paid nearly **$100,000 more** in taxes (17% more) than the average U.S.-born person over the 30-year period

**Methodology note:** The Cato study builds upon the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (NASEM) fiscal effects model and analyzes three decades of federal, state, and local government budgets.

**(SOURCED)** NAS (2017): While first-generation immigrants cost more in government services, second-generation immigrants are among the strongest fiscal and economic contributors in the U.S.

Sources:
- [Cato Institute: Immigrants Reduced Deficits by $14.5 Trillion](https://www.cato.org/blog/cato-study-immigrants-reduced-deficits-145-trillion-1994)
- [Cato Institute: Immigrants' Recent Effects on Government Budgets: 1994-2023 (White Paper)](https://www.cato.org/white-paper/immigrants-recent-effects-government-budgets-1994-2023)
- [Fortune: How the undocumented actually shrank the deficit by $14.5 trillion](https://fortune.com/2026/02/03/how-much-do-immigrants-contribute-to-us-economy-cato-institute-subsidized-deficit-national-debt/)

### 5.3 Claim: "We Can Innovate Without Immigrants"

**Rating: LARGELY FALSE**

**(SOURCED)** NBER/Stanford (Bernstein, Diamond, et al., 2022):
- Immigrants constitute **16% of all U.S. inventors** but produce **36% of total innovation output** (direct patents + collaborative spillovers)
- Immigrants are responsible for **23% of patents** issued between 1990 and 2016
- Co-inventor productivity drops **17%** after the death of an immigrant collaborator (vs. 9% for native collaborators) -- demonstrating outsized knowledge spillover effects
- Immigrants are **10% more likely** to cite international work and **twice as likely** to collaborate with foreign inventors

**(SOURCED)** Moser and San (2020): The 1920s immigration restrictions led to a 68% decline in U.S. invention in affected fields, with effects lasting approximately 40 years (see Section 3).

**Why natives cannot simply replace immigrant inventors:** The NBER research shows immigrant inventors operate in cutting-edge, rapidly changing sectors and bring unique international knowledge networks. Their contribution is not merely additive but multiplicative through collaboration effects.

Sources:
- [NBER: The Outsize Role of Immigrants in US Innovation](https://www.nber.org/digest/20233/outsize-role-immigrants-us-innovation)
- [NBER: The Contribution of High-Skilled Immigrants to Innovation (Working Paper 30797)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30797)
- [Stanford GSB: A New Look at Immigrants' Outsize Contribution to Innovation](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/new-look-immigrants-outsize-contribution-innovation-us)

### 5.4 Claim: "Restricting Immigration Protects American Jobs"

**Rating: LARGELY FALSE**

**(SOURCED)** Britta Glennon, NBER Working Paper 27538 / Management Science (2023):
- When firms cannot fill H-1B positions, they do NOT hire more Americans -- they offshore
- Average: **0.3 jobs** created at foreign affiliates for every unfilled H-1B position
- Most globalized MNCs: **0.9 jobs** created abroad for every visa rejection
- Firms historically dependent on H-1B workers increased foreign affiliate employment by **27% more** than less-dependent firms after the 2004 visa cap reduction
- Jobs flow primarily to **India, China, and Canada**
- Effects are strongest among **R&D-intensive firms** (which account for 80% of domestic R&D)

**The paradox:** Restricting H-1B visas does not create American jobs. It moves jobs overseas while reducing domestic innovation capacity.

Sources:
- [NBER: Restricting Visas for Skilled Workers Leads to Offshoring](https://www.nber.org/digest/sep20/restricting-visas-skilled-workers-leads-offshoring)
- [NBER Working Paper 27538](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27538)
- [Management Science: How Do Restrictions on High-Skilled Immigration Affect Offshoring?](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2023.4715)

### 5.5 Claim: "H-1B Workers Suppress Wages"

**Rating: MIXED**

**(SOURCED)** Evidence supporting concern:
- 60% of H-1B positions are assigned wage levels below local median wage (EPI analysis)
- Some computer science subfields saw 2.6%-5.1% lower wages from H-1B immigration (NBER)
- Outsourcing firms have been documented using the program for below-market labor

**(SOURCED)** Evidence against broad claim:
- Economy-wide, immigration had a **positive effect (+1.7% to +2.6%)** on wages of less-educated native workers over 2000-2019 (NBER)
- Companies winning H-1B lotteries achieve higher job growth, productivity, and profit margins
- Reducing H-1B supply by 10% lowers annual native welfare by ~$2.9 billion (Richmond Fed)

**Verdict:** Wage suppression exists at the firm level due to program design flaws (the prevailing wage system), but is not an economy-wide phenomenon. The evidence supports reform of wage floors, not elimination of the program.

Sources:
- [EPI: H-1B visas and prevailing wage levels](https://www.epi.org/publication/h-1b-visas-and-prevailing-wage-levels/)
- [NBER: Immigration's Effect on US Wages (Working Paper 32389)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32389/w32389.pdf)
- [Richmond Fed: H-1B Visa Program Changes](https://www.richmondfed.org/publications/research/economic_brief/2025/eb_25-39)

### 5.6 Claim: "Too Many Immigrants Lower Innovation Quality"

**Rating: MIXED**

**(SOURCED)** Evidence supporting concern:
- China now issues over 1 million patents annually (3x the U.S.), suggesting volume alone is not the metric
- Some argue that indiscriminate expansion could dilute quality

**(SOURCED)** Evidence against:
- U.S. still leads in mean patent importance across major technology fields (CSIS)
- U.S. leads in international patent families (patents filed in 2+ countries), a proxy for high-value inventions
- Immigrant inventors are disproportionately represented in the most-cited patents (25% of the most-cited patents)

Source: [CSIS: What Can Patent Data Reveal About US-China Technology Competition](https://www.csis.org/analysis/what-can-patent-data-reveal-about-us-china-technology-competition)

## 7. The Offshoring Paradox

### 7.1 Core Finding

**Source:** Britta Glennon, "How Do Restrictions on High-Skilled Immigration Affect Offshoring? Evidence from the H-1B Program," NBER Working Paper 27538 (2020); published in Management Science (2023).
**Methodology:** Linked H-1B visa lottery data with Bureau of Economic Analysis data on U.S. multinational corporations and their foreign affiliates. Exploited the 2007-2008 H-1B visa lottery as a natural experiment.

**(SOURCED)** Key findings:
- A 1-percentage-point reduction in H-1B supply (relative to initial employment) caused foreign affiliate growth rates to increase by **12-16%**
- This translates to approximately **0.3 jobs created at foreign affiliates** for every unfilled H-1B position (average across all MNCs)
- The most globalized MNCs hire **0.9 employees abroad** for every visa rejection
- Firms historically dependent on H-1B workers increased foreign affiliate employment by **27% more** than less-dependent firms following the 2004 cap reduction
- Multinational corporations are responsible for **80% of domestic R&D**

### 7.2 Geographic Distribution of Offshored Jobs

**(SOURCED)** Jobs flow primarily to three destinations:
1. **India** -- largest pre-existing pool of H-1B-caliber talent
2. **China** -- historical source of H-1B workers
3. **Canada** -- proximity advantage; relaxed immigration rules (e.g., Microsoft Vancouver expansion)

### 7.3 The Microsoft Vancouver Case

**(SOURCED)** Microsoft, headquartered in Redmond, Washington, expanded its Vancouver, British Columbia operations specifically because Canada's immigration rules made it easier to hire foreign talent that could not obtain U.S. H-1B visas. Vancouver is approximately 3 hours from Microsoft's headquarters, making it a convenient alternative for talent the company could not bring to the U.S.

Sources:
- [NBER Digest: Restricting Visas for Skilled Workers Leads to Offshoring](https://www.nber.org/digest/sep20/restricting-visas-skilled-workers-leads-offshoring)
- [NBER Working Paper 27538 (PDF)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w27538/w27538.pdf)
- [Management Science: H-1B Program and Offshoring](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2023.4715)
