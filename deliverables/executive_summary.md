# Built by Immigrants, Threatened by Policy
## Executive Summary: The Impact of Immigrant Entrepreneurs on the U.S. Innovation Economy

**Harvard University · Data Visualization Group Project · Spring 2025**

> **Scope note:** This executive summary presents **descriptive and exploratory analysis** — mapping the scale, composition, and geographic distribution of immigrant entrepreneurship and overlaying policy shifts as contextual factors. We do not attempt causal inference or predictive economic modelling; projections from existing studies (NFAP, Brookings, Hoover, Cato) are cited as reference points, not original analysis.

---

## The Big Picture

Immigrants represent approximately **14-15% of the U.S. population**, yet their association with American innovation is consistently disproportionate across multiple independent datasets:

| Metric | Immigrant Contribution | Multiple vs. Population Share | Source |
|--------|----------------------|------------------------------|--------|
| Fortune 500 founders (incl. 2nd gen) | **46.2%** (231 of 500) | 3.2x | AIC 2025 |
| Unicorn startup founders | **55%** (319 of 582) | 3.8x | NFAP 2022 |
| U.S. patent innovation | **36%** (direct + collaborative)* | 2.5x | NBER WP 30797 |
| New entrepreneurs | **~25%** | ~1.8x | Kauffman 2019 |
| AI startup founders | **60-66%** | ~4.3x | CSET/NFAP/IFP |

*Patent figures cited from NBER published analysis; inventor nationality is not directly recorded in USPTO filings — see methodological notes in full research.

**Caveat:** These metrics come from different sources, use different definitions of "immigrant" (some include 2nd generation, others do not), and cover different time periods. The pattern is consistent but metrics are not directly comparable.

## Act 2 — Scale: An Undeniable Economic Force

### Fortune 500 (Source: AIC 2025)
- **231 of 500** Fortune 500 companies were founded by immigrants or their children (46.2%)
  - 109 by first-generation immigrants; 122 by children of immigrants
- Combined annual revenue: **$8.6 trillion** — equivalent to the world's 3rd-largest economy
- Combined employees: **15.4 million** worldwide
- Highest concentration: Professional services (80%), Manufacturing (65.6%), Information/Tech (57.5%)

**Caveat:** The 46.2% figure includes both first- and second-generation Americans. Revenue represents total company revenue, not revenue attributable to the founder's immigrant status.

### Unicorn Startups ($1B+ Valuation) (Source: NFAP 2022)
- **319 of 582** U.S. unicorns have at least one immigrant founder (55%)
- Collective value: **$1.2 trillion+**
- Growth: From 50 immigrant-founded unicorns in 2018 to 319 in 2022 — a **538% increase**
- Founders from **57 countries** of origin

**Caveat:** Valuations are private-market estimates from 2022 venture capital boom; many have shifted since. "Founder" includes companies with at least one immigrant co-founder.

### Entrepreneurship Rate
- Immigrants are **80% more likely** to found a firm than native-born Americans (Azoulay et al. / MIT, 2022; based on firm formations 2005-2010)
- Immigrants start businesses at **2.4x the native-born rate** (Kauffman Foundation, 2023)
- Immigrant-founded firms create **42% more jobs** relative to population share

**Caveat:** The 2.4x gap partly reflects the native-born rate declining (from 0.34% to 0.28%), not just the immigrant rate rising.

### Patent Innovation (Source: NBER Working Paper 30797)
- Immigrants are 16% of U.S. inventors but produce **23% of all patents** (1990-2016)
- Including collaborative work with native-born inventors, immigrants account for **36% of total U.S. innovation**
- Citation-weighted and market-value-weighted shares are even higher (24-25%)

**Critical caveat:** Inventor nationality is not directly recorded in USPTO filings. The NBER researchers used age-of-SSN-assignment as a proxy — an innovative but imperfect identification strategy. We cite their published findings, not independent verification.

## Act 4 — The Policy Context: Descriptive Overlay

> **Methodological note:** We present temporal co-occurrence between policy changes and shifts in denial rates, processing backlogs, and talent-flow indicators. We do not claim that any single policy change caused a specific change in startup founding rates. External factors — macroeconomic cycles, VC market conditions, COVID-19 — also affect these metrics.

### H-1B Denial Rate by Policy Era

| Era | Denial Rate Range | Key Policy |
|-----|------------------|-----------|
| Obama (2010-2016) | 5-10% | DACA, STEM OPT expansion, H-4 EAD |
| Trump 1st Term (2017-2020) | **13-24%** | "Buy American, Hire American," Travel Bans |
| Biden (2021-2024) | 2-3.5% | H-1B modernization, BAHA rescission |
| Trump 2nd Term (2025-) | 2.8% (rising expected) | **$100,000 H-1B fee**, 75-country visa pause |

### Key Policy Events
- **2017**: "Buy American, Hire American" Executive Order; H-1B denial rate rose from 6% to 24% (FY 2016 → FY 2018)
- **2017-2020**: Travel bans affecting 13+ countries; Request for Evidence (RFE) rate peaked at 40.2%
- **2020**: ITServe Alliance v. Cissna settlement — key USCIS memo rescinded
- **2021**: Biden rescission of BAHA; USCIS Policy Alert PA-2021-05 restores deference to prior approvals
- **2025**: $100,000 H-1B petition fee (a ~32-41x increase); immigrant visa processing paused for 75 countries
- **Green Card Backlog**: 1.8 million in employment-based backlog (Cato, 2024); India EB-2 wait: **12-18 years**; theoretical 134-year wait for new EB-2 India applicants (Boundless, 2024)

### Brain Drain Indicators
*Note: These indicators describe talent movement patterns but cannot isolate immigration policy as the sole driver. Economic opportunity, family considerations, and home-country conditions are also factors.*

- **82%** of U.S. employers reported losing foreign employees due to visa issues (Envoy Global, 2023)
- **1 million+** Indians renounced citizenship between 2019-2024 (required when obtaining citizenship elsewhere; India does not permit dual citizenship)
- **75%** of scientists surveyed considering leaving the U.S. (Nature, 2025 — convenience sample of 1,600)
- **+41%** increase in U.S. job seekers applying to Canada (Nature jobs board, early 2025 vs. 2024)
- European Research Council grant applications from U.S. researchers nearly tripled: 60 (2024) → 116 (2025) → 169 (2026)
- Canada's Start-Up Visa program: record 5,595 PRs granted in 2024 (but Canada itself is now capping intake at 1,000/yr by 2026-2027)

## Act 5 — What's at Stake: Cited Projections and Historical Evidence

> **Note:** All projections below are attributed to their source studies. We present these as existing estimates from cited sources, not original modelling. Each source's methodology is noted.

### Projected Costs of Continued Immigration Restrictions (2025-2035)

| Impact Area | Projected Impact | Classification | Source |
|-------------|-----------------|----------------|--------|
| GDP | **-$12.1 trillion** cumulative | Cited projection | NFAP Oct 2025 |
| Workforce | **-15.7 million** workers by 2035 | Cited projection | NFAP Oct 2025 |
| Federal debt | **+$1.74 trillion** additional | Cited projection | NFAP Oct 2025 |
| Monthly job creation (2026) | **-20K to +50K/mo** (vs. ~140-180K in 2024) | Cited projection | Brookings Jan 2026 |
| Consumer spending | **-$60 to -$110 billion** over 2 years | Cited projection | Brookings Jan 2026 |

### Historical Precedent: The Immigration Act of 1924 (Source: Moser & San, AEA 2020)
When the U.S. restricted immigration in the 1920s:
- Immigration fell from ~360,000/year to ~165,000/year
- U.S. invention in affected (Eastern/Southern European) fields declined **68%**
- Native-born scientist patent production in those fields declined **45%**
- **16% additional** fields had zero patents after quotas
- The effects persisted for **~40 years** (into the 1960s)
- An estimated **1,170 scientists** were "missing" from U.S. science by the 1950s

**Critical insight:** The decline was not limited to immigrant scientists themselves. Native-born American scientists in the same fields also became dramatically less productive, demonstrating positive spillover effects from immigrant colleagues.

### Global Competition for Talent

| Country | Program | Processing Time | Startup Visa? | Selection Method |
|---------|---------|----------------|---------------|-----------------|
| **United States** | H-1B (lottery) | **6-9 months** | **No** | Random lottery |
| Canada | Global Talent Stream | **2 weeks** | Yes (paused; successor launching) | Merit/employer-driven |
| United Kingdom | Global Talent Visa | **2-4 months** | Via Global Talent pathway | Merit/endorsement; no cap |
| Germany | Chancenkarte | **2-12 weeks** | Limited | Points-based |
| Singapore | Tech.Pass / ONE Pass | **4-8 weeks** | Yes (EntrePass) | Merit/salary threshold |

### The Opportunity Cost of Reform
If the U.S. expanded H-1B visas (per Hoover Institution modelling of Senate Gang of Eight proposal):
- **+$456 billion** in additional GDP over 10 years (Hoover — H-1B expansion only; not comparable to NFAP restriction scenario)
- **+$113 billion** in additional federal tax revenue (Hoover)
- Immigrant fiscal surplus (1994-2023): **$14.5 trillion** (Cato Institute — immigrants paid 14% of tax revenue but accounted for only 7% of spending)

### The Offshoring Paradox (Source: Glennon, NBER WP 27538 / Management Science 2023)
When companies can't hire foreign talent in the U.S., they do NOT hire more Americans — they offshore:
- Average firm: **0.3 jobs created abroad** per unfilled H-1B position
- Most globalized firms: **0.9 jobs offshored** per unfilled H-1B
- Firms historically dependent on H-1B workers increased foreign affiliate employment by **27% more** than less-dependent firms after the 2004 cap reduction
- Microsoft expanded Vancouver operations specifically because Canada's immigration rules made it easier to hire talent denied U.S. H-1B visas

## Key Takeaways for Policymakers

1. **Immigrants are associated with a disproportionate share of American innovation.** Immigrant-founded Fortune 500 companies employ 15.4 million people and generate $8.6 trillion in revenue. 55% of billion-dollar startups have an immigrant founder.

2. **Historical evidence shows immigration restrictions can persistently reduce innovation capacity.** The 1920s restriction led to a 68% decline in invention in affected fields, lasting ~40 years — and native-born scientists were also less productive when immigrant colleagues were excluded (Moser & San).

3. **Competitor nations are actively recruiting the talent America's system makes difficult to retain.** Canada (2-week processing), UK (no cap, merit-based), Germany, and Singapore have all created fast-track programs for high-skilled talent.

4. **Published projections suggest significant economic costs from continued restriction.** NFAP estimates $12.1 trillion in GDP, 15.7 million workers, and $1.74 trillion in additional federal debt at risk over the next decade. These are cited projections, not our original modelling.

5. **Evidence suggests reform would generate fiscal returns.** H-1B expansion alone could generate $456 billion in GDP and $113 billion in tax revenue over 10 years (Hoover). Immigrants have generated a $14.5 trillion fiscal surplus since 1994 (Cato).

## Methodological Integrity Note

This executive summary adheres to the following principles:
- **All analysis is descriptive and exploratory.** No causal claims are made.
- **All projections are attributed** to their source studies with methodology noted.
- **Data limitations are flagged** — survivorship bias, definition differences, and small-sample caveats.
- **Temporal co-occurrence ≠ causation.** Policy changes are presented alongside metric shifts, not as their proven cause.
