# Peer Critique Response

## What We Learned from Peer Feedback

- **Geographic data demands spatial encoding**: Peers pointed out that state-level data shown as bar charts fails to leverage spatial intuition — map-based visualization is far more effective for communicating geographic concentration patterns.

- **Too many visualizations without connective tissue overwhelms**: With 15–20 charts, reviewers felt the narrative got lost. The issue wasn't the quantity itself, but the lack of structure guiding readers through the story.

- **Counterarguments need to be preempted, not ignored**: The "immigrants take American jobs" critique is predictable, and leaving it unaddressed weakens credibility. Peers pushed us to confront skepticism directly with data (e.g., Glennon's offshoring findings, NBER labor complementarity research).

- **Country × sector specialization is a compelling narrative hook**: Reviewers found the idea that specific nationalities dominate specific industries (India → AI/SaaS, Israel → cybersecurity, Taiwan → semiconductors) particularly engaging — it turns abstract statistics into concrete, memorable patterns.

- **Static visualizations limit engagement**: Feedback indicated that users want to explore the data themselves, not just read it passively. Interactivity transforms a presentation into an experience.

## What We Changed

- **Added interactive maps**: Replaced static bar charts with interactive choropleth and bubble-overlay maps for U.S. state-level and world origin data, allowing hover, click, and exploration.

- **Added a table of contents**: Introduced structured navigation so readers can orient themselves within the 5-act narrative and jump to specific sections without getting lost in 15+ visualizations.

- **Rebuilt the timeline as an explorable interactive**: The Act 4 policy timeline was previously static. It is now an interactive timeline where users can click into specific eras (Obama, Trump, Biden) to see corresponding H-1B denial rates, RFE spikes, and policy events in context.
